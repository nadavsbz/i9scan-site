I-9 fines in 2026: $288 to $2,861 per form

Paperwork fines are charged per form, not per inspection. Here is the range in force today, why it didn't rise in 2026, and how ICE decides where in that range your business lands.

I9Scan guide · Updated September 15, 2026 · Not legal advice

In short

  • $288 minimum, $2,861 maximum for each Form I-9 with a paperwork violation.
  • These amounts were set on January 2, 2025. There was no 2026 inflation adjustment.
  • ICE starts from the share of your forms with violations, then adjusts for five factors by up to 25% in either direction.
  • Knowingly hiring or continuing to employ unauthorized workers carries separate, higher penalties.

The per-form range

Violation typeMinimum per formMaximum per formIn force since
Form I-9 paperwork violations$288$2,86101/02/2025

Civil penalties are normally adjusted for inflation every year. In April 2026 the Office of Management and Budget cancelled the 2026 adjustment, so the amounts published in January 2025 still apply to penalties assessed in 2026.

How ICE sets the amount

Step 1: the share of forms with violations

After an inspection, ICE divides the number of forms with violations by the number of forms it inspected. A higher share, and prior violations, push the starting amount per form toward the top of the range.

Step 2: five factors

ICE then adjusts that starting amount using five factors. Each one can add 5%, subtract 5% or change nothing, and the total adjustment is capped at 25% either way.

FactorWhat tends to helpWhat tends to hurt
Size of the businessSmall businessLarge business
Good faith of the employerSelf-audits, corrections, trainingIgnoring known problems
Seriousness of the violationsMinor omissionsForms never completed
Unauthorized workers involvedNone foundUnauthorized workers on the payroll
History of previous violationsNo prior violationsEarlier fines or warnings

The "helps" and "hurts" columns are general tendencies, not ICE's published criteria.

A worked example

A restaurant keeps 40 forms. An inspection finds substantive violations on 12 of them, 30% of the file.

The real amount falls between those numbers, depending on the violation share and the five factors.

Why the March 2026 change matters for fines

Technical errors come with at least 10 business days to fix after ICE points them out, so they rarely turn into fines. On March 16, 2026 ICE moved more than ten common errors to the substantive list, where there is no correction window. The same file can now produce a much larger fine than it would have a year ago. The full list is in our guide to the rule change.

How to lower your exposure before an inspection

  1. Run a self-audit of every form you are required to keep.
  2. Correct errors the way USCIS describes, with today's date and your initials.
  3. Keep a memo of what you reviewed and fixed. It is evidence of good faith.
  4. Know which old forms you may discard. The USCIS Handbook for Employers (M-274) explains the retention period.
